# H&S audit import — Q4 2025 and Q1 2026

Source: `HS_AUDIT_FINDINGS.xlsx`, read by `scripts/import-hs-audit.py` into
`data/hs-audits-2025Q4-2026Q1.json` (this file's audit trail) and turned into
the app's dataset by `scripts/build-recorded-dataset.py`
(`src/lib/ehss/recorded.ts`).

20 blocks across 2 quarters and 2 sub-regions; **19 audits imported**, one
dropped as a duplicate. This is now the app's only dataset — the generated
demo data is gone from the app and survives only as a test fixture
(`src/lib/ehss/fixture.ts`), because the question-level machinery still needs
something with answers in it to test against.

## What came in, and what did not

**In:** the 15 checklist area scores per audit (section A, B1–B12, C1, C2) as
raw points, the stated total, project number, audit date, inspection number
and sub-region.

**Verification:** run
`python3 scripts/verify-against-sheet.py <workbook> src/lib/ehss/recorded.ts`
on every new quarter before trusting an import. It re-reads the workbook by
searching for the sheet's own labels rather than fixed row offsets, so it
disagrees with the importer if next quarter's template shifts a row — instead
of silently importing the wrong cells. Last run: **PASS**, 279 area cells and
76 metadata fields identical to the sheet.

**Not in, deliberately:**

- **Auditor names and mobile numbers** (row 3 of every block). Importing them
  would put personal data in the app, which invalidates the PDPL position in
  `COMPLIANCE-KSA.md` §2 *and* the determination in §4 that lets this data be
  hosted outside the Kingdom.
- **The PART 3 narrative.** It is one free-text paragraph per question,
  mixing positive and negative commentary, with no machine-readable answer or
  cause. Deriving findings from it would mean interpreting prose, and the
  guesses would flow into the recurrence and category analytics as if they
  were recorded fact.

**Therefore unavailable for these two quarters:** per-question Full/Partial/No
answers, which questions failed, and why. The findings register, the SHEW
heat-map, the issue categories and question-level recurrence have nothing to
show until audits are entered through the app itself.

## Scoring: percentages recomputed from points

Re-verified against the workbook on 2026-10-10 by
`scripts/verify-against-sheet.py`, which re-reads the file independently of
the importer. Findings below are from that run.

**There are no formulas in the workbook.** Every cell — points, percentages,
section scores, the stated total — is a hardcoded number. Nothing recalculates.
An earlier note here blamed "a stale denominator in the formula"; that was
wrong, and it matters, because there is no formula to repair. The percentage
cells were typed or pasted, and they have drifted from the points beside them.

**The points columns, by contrast, are completely sound:**

- All 40 section point-sums add up exactly — every section row equals the sum
  of its own sub-sections, in both possible and scored.
- Every scored value is a clean half-point step.
- One value only is out of range: ECH Q1 2026 C2 scored 21.5 against 21
  possible. The importer caps it at 21 and records it.

**The percentage cells are wrong in 32 places** (95 others differ only in the
second decimal, which is display rounding and harmless):

| Area | Percentage cells genuinely wrong |
|---|---:|
| A, B1–B5, B7–B12 | **0** |
| B6 Subcontractor Management | 4 |
| C1 Leadership Engagement | 8 |
| C2 Planning | **20 — every audit** |

C2 is the one with a traceable cause: in 9 of the 20 it divides by 25 — the
checklist's **full** nominal weight — while the possible column says 21,
i.e. the percentage ignores the N/A questions that the points correctly
exclude. The rest (divisors of 22, 23, 24) and all the C1 and B6 errors follow
no pattern at all.

So the importer reads points and recomputes the percentage with the workbook's
documented rule: points ÷ applicable weight.

### The section rows and stated totals follow no rule either

Checked every roll-up rule against the sheet's own numbers:

- **Section B** matches points ÷ possible in 4 of 20 blocks and nothing in 16.
- **Section C** matches the mean of its sub-section cells in 9 and nothing
  in 11.
- **The stated TOTAL is explained by no rule in 11 of 20 blocks** — not the
  mean of the section cells, not the mean of the 15 areas, not total points
  over total possible, not the app's own rule.

The two sections do not even agree with each other on which rule to use. These
are typed figures, so **there is nothing to reconcile to** — which is the
mechanical reason the stated totals cannot be treated as a record, beyond the
owner's instruction that they are not one.

**The stated percentages are not a record** (owner's instruction,
2026-10-07): the app follows the points, and the figures below are recorded
here so the source sheet can be fixed, not so the app can reconcile to them.

**This changes reported scores, in nearly every audit.** Recomputed from the
sheet's own points, the total differs from the total the sheet stated in **18
of the 19** imported audits:

- **9 overstate** performance, by up to **14.2 points** (RPCO Q1 2026 was
  reported at 60% against 45.8% from its points; ECH Q1 2026 at 85% against
  74.4%; NBC at 60% against 50.9%).
- **9 understate** it, by up to 4.3 points.
- 1 agrees within half a point.
- Every error above 5 points is an **overstatement**.

The two quarters already reported to management are therefore wrong in both
directions, mostly flattering. Because the totals are typed rather than
derived, the errors are not a single fault propagating — each is its own.

## One block is a copy of another

The block headed **"SIBS 838"** in Q4 2025 (dated 12 November, no work order)
holds **exactly** PPCO's Q4 2025 figures: the same 15 scored values, the same
15 applicable weights, and the same stated total of 62 — for an audit dated a
month apart. Two audits of two contractors on two dates cannot agree on 30
numbers, so this is PPCO's row pasted under SIBS's name.

It is **excluded** from the dataset (named explicitly in
`scripts/build-recorded-dataset.py`, and pinned in `recorded.test.ts`).
Importing it would have given SIBS PPCO's score as its own November audit and
split SIBS's trend across a number it never earned.

**If SIBS was in fact audited on 12 November 2025, those figures need
re-supplying** — they are not recoverable from this file.

## Contractor identity — settled: initials and the last three digits

Contractors are identified in the app as **initials + the last three digits of
the work order** — `SIBS (838)`, `AF (272)`, `ABYA (134)` — and nothing more.
Full work orders and names as typed stay in `data/`, which is not bundled into
the app.

Two things fall out of that:

- It is deliberately **unidentifying**, which is what keeps the determination
  in `COMPLIANCE-KSA.md` §4 true: the data may be hosted outside the Kingdom
  *because* it names nobody and nothing.
- It **settles the spelling problems by construction**. The last three digits
  make `4800000838`, `800000838` (a digit short) and `SIBS 838` one
  contractor, which is what they are; Al-Biriki's nine-digit `480000901` lands
  on `901` whether or not a leading 4 was dropped. The unnumbered OXAGON
  Terminal 2 block has no work order at all, so it carries its initials alone:
  `OT2 (D&B)`.

Thirteen contractors result. The judgement calls — which letters make up the
initials — are written down in the generator rather than derived, because an
algorithm would have turned the ECH joint venture ("JV ECH T1") into "JET".

## What the app does with the imported data

Each audit carries the 15 **area** scores as points, not per-question answers,
so:

- **Works now:** the league table and programme average, contractor
  trajectories, the contractor × checklist-area matrix, and the dashboard
  drill-down down to the area level (level 1 and level 2).
- **Empty until audits are entered through the app:** the gap register, the
  SHEW heat-map, issue categories, per-question recurrence and the question
  rankings. Every one of those views now says *why* it is empty rather than
  showing a bare "nothing in scope" — an empty findings register otherwise
  reads as a clean audit, and these contractors average 64.9%.
- **Not run at all for these quarters:** Critical Risk Control, Environment,
  Security and Worker Welfare. The weighted overall therefore renormalizes
  onto Health & Safety alone rather than scoring a contractor down for audits
  nobody performed.

The weakest areas programme-wide, from the imported data: **B6 Subcontractor
Management at 25%** (13 of 13 contractors below target), **C1 Leadership
Engagement at 51%** (13 of 13), **B7 Training at 55%** (13 of 13) and
**section A Management at 56%** (13 of 13).
